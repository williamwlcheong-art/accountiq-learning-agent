Overview
Introduction
This report sets out the valuation rationale in respect of the proposed related-party acquisition of the
accommodation-management business operated by Marina Terrace Accommodation Limited ("MTAL") under
the contemplated Heads of Agreement.

The purpose of the valuation is to assess the going-concern value of MTAL on a notional arm’s-length basis. In
doing so, the existing management arrangement with All About The View Limited ("AATV") has been treated
as quasi-owner remuneration and normalised, so that the resulting value indication reflects the consideration
that an independent third-party purchaser could reasonably be expected to pay for the business.

The valuation has been prepared using a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) methodology, which is considered
appropriate for assessing the present value of future maintainable cash flows generated by the business as a
going concern. The analysis is expressed on a fair market value basis, assuming a notional transaction between
a willing buyer and a willing seller dealing at arm’s length.

Background
MTAL operates within the Wanaka short-stay accommodation market, which has exhibited stable post-COVID
demand supported by the town’s lakeside setting, year-round tourism appeal, and constrained supply of
professionally managed apartment accommodation.

Since its incorporation in May 2016, MTAL has operated as both Building Manager and Letting Manager for the
Marina Terrace complex and has progressively expanded its managed portfolio to 14 apartments across the
Spinnaker, Admiral, Nautilus Townside, Nautilus Northside, Navigator, Quayside and Anchorage buildings.

MTAL is owned by eight shareholders holding 110 shares in aggregate. The largest shareholdings are Tallon
Automotive Limited (29%), T G Tuck (20%) and Miller Financial Services (14%). Governance is overseen by
director M L Tuck, while day-to-day operations are managed by Karen Mackenzie through AATV.
The current operating platform is underpinned by several key features:

    •   a 10-year Body Corporate management contract executed on 23 December 2020 with two 10-year
        rights of renewal, providing contractual coverage to 2050;

    •   an office and reception lease executed on 12 February 2021 at $0.013m + GST per annum, CPI-
        indexed;

    •   12 of 14 apartments are subject to formal Apartment Management Agreements; the remaining two
        operate without a formal agreement; and

    •   an established Wanaka tourism and accommodation market with stable post-COVID demand.

On completion of the proposed Heads of Agreement, the existing related-party management arrangement
between MTAL and AATV—under which AATV currently receives 50% of MTAL’s pre-fee profit—will cease, as
AATV will become the owner of MTAL.

Having now reached its 14-apartment operating footprint, MTAL has demonstrated a level of earnings stability
sufficient to support the use of FY2026 as the base year for assessing sustainable maintainable earnings.

Taken together, these contractual arrangements support both the continuity of the operating platform and
the retention rationale for the current apartment owners. Under the proposed Heads of Agreement, AATV
would step into the existing shareholder position, such that the contractual framework and operating platform
would remain with the company.

Valuation
methodology adopted
Fundamental to a company valuation is to determine the appropriate valuation
method. In assessing Marina Terrace Accommodation Limited value, the
Discounted Cash Flow model has been used. We believe this is the most
appropriate valuation methodology for the reasons set out below.

Further, we have assessed the value of Marina Terrace Accommodation Limited
on a Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) basis: the cash flows are unlevered (interest
excluded) and are stated after corporate tax, and are discounted at the Weighted
Average Cost of Capital (WACC), consistent with accepted valuation principles.

Free cash flow to firm is derived from normalised maintainable EBITDA by
deducting corporate tax at 28% and any incremental capital expenditure and
movement in working capital.

In this engagement no further capital expenditure (beyond routine maintenance)
and no change in working capital are envisaged, and depreciation on the modest
fixed-asset base is negligible, so that FCFF approximates normalised EBITDA less
corporate tax at 28% (i.e. approximately 72% of EBITDA).


Discounted Cash Flow
DCF is a direct valuation technique that values a company by projecting its future cash flows and then
uses the Net Present Value (NPV) method to value those cash flows.

In the DCF analysis, the cash flows are projected by using a series of assumptions about how the business
will perform in the future, and then forecasting how this business performance translates into the cash
flow generated by the business. The DCF method should be used to provide an indicative value Marina
Terrace Accommodation Limited as it attempts to measure the value created by a business directly. It is
thus the most theoretically correct valuation method available; the value of a firm ultimately derives from
the inherent value of its future cash flows to its equity holders.

DCF is the most broadly used valuation technique, simply because of its theoretical underpinnings and its
ability to be used in almost all scenarios. DCF is used by Investment Bankers, Corporate Finance
professionals undertaking entity valuations and mergers and acquisitions, Business Development
professionals, and Academics. NPV is a mathematical technique used in DCF modelling translating
projected annual cash flow amounts into risk adjusted today equivalent values so that each year’s
projected cash flows can be summed up in comparable, current-dollar amounts.

Basis of Valuation

The valuation has been prepared on a going-concern basis, reflecting the business’s continued operation
at the valuation date. The valuation has been undertaken for commercial discussions between the existing
shareholders of MTAL and the proposed related-party purchaser, AATV, and is expressed on a fair market
value basis, assuming a notional, arm’s-length transaction between a willing but not anxious buyer and a
willing but not anxious seller.

In applying this basis, the business is assessed as an operating enterprise, rather than on a liquidation or
break-up basis. The valuation focuses on the economic benefits expected to be derived from the business
as conducted at the valuation date, having regard to the conditions under which it has operated.



Valuation Methodology Adopted

The valuation has been carried out using a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) methodology. Under this
approach, the value of the business is assessed by estimating future maintainable after-tax cash flows
(free cash flow to firm, derived from normalised EBITDA less corporate tax at 28%) and discounting those
cash flows to their present value using an appropriate discount rate that reflects time value and business
risk.
The DCF method has been adopted as it is considered appropriate in circumstances where:

    •   the business has a long trading history supported by detailed financial information;

    •   future performance can be assessed by reference to historical operating results and observable
        operating conditions; and

    •   the valuation purpose requires assessment of fair-market enterprise value supporting commercial
        discussions between the existing shareholders and the proposed related-party purchaser.

Sales Trends:
   •   Total income grew from $0.047m in FY22 to $0.428m in FY23, $0.847m in FY24, $1.197m in FY25,

   •   The key structural point sits at the FY23/FY24 boundary. In FY23, accommodation income was

   •   The total is unaﬀected by the change; the +97.7% FY23→FY24 movement reﬂects genuine growth

   •    Growth moderated as the business scaled, from +97.7% (FY23→FY24) to +41.3% (FY24→FY25) and
       +13.3% (FY25→FY26), consistent with a maturing revenue base. FY26 has accordingly been
       adopted as the base year for sustainable maintainable earnings.
   •   For forecasting purposes, FY2026 has been treated as the actual base year, with revenue
       projected to grow at 3.1% per annum from FY2027 onward, in line with inflationary growth.
   •   In our view, the current earnings base reflects a business that has reached a more stable
       operating scale under the existing 14-apartment footprint and contractual arrangements.

A material related-party arrangement exists between MTAL and All About The View Limited (AATV), the

Key facts

    -   AATV is wholly owned by Karen Mackenzie and provides General Manager and operational services
        to MTAL.
    -   The related-party fee paid to AATV is 50% of MTAL’s pre-fee profit. AATV-related payable on the
        FY24 balance sheet was $0.107m and on the FY25 balance sheet was $0.154m.
    -   On a notional arm’s-length basis, MTAL would not pay 50% of pre-fee profit to a third-party General

take out the market risk and have this Beta reflect 100% of the private entity’s specific risk. We have adopted

provides a broader and more statistically robust basis than a small local-market regression.

Equity return
An equity return of 12.64% has been adopted as the required rate of return for a purchaser of this small,
owner-operated accommodation management business. This rate reflects the company's limited scale,
dependence on the General Manager's ongoing involvement, exposure to tourism market conditions and
online travel agency ("OTA") dynamics, and the illiquid nature of a privately held business of this type.

Additional risk factors include the concentration of operations within a single apartment complex in Wanaka,
reliance on related parties for management services, and a relatively concentrated revenue base. The top
three apartments are expected to generate approximately $0.610m of FY2026 letting revenue, increasing the
business's exposure to the retention and performance of a small number of apartment owners.

A significant component of the risk assessment relates to contractual security. While the Apartment
Management Agreements have stated terms of up to 30 years, apartment owners retain the right to terminate

position toward the bottom of the range remains consistent with MTAL's materially shorter effective
contractual tenure (about six months under clause 7.6) and revenue concentration.

These multiples reflect the modest scale of the operation, single-location concentration on the Marina Terrace
complex, and the small number of key contracts.



Market Evidence
As part of our assessment, we have considered a recent transaction involving the sale of a comparable
apartment management and letting business in Queenstown. The business managed 19 apartments and
transacted for $1.95 million, implying a multiple of approximately 4.0x EBITDA/SDE based on reported
earnings of $0.483m.

The transaction provides a useful market reference point given its exposure to the short-term accommodation
sector and broadly comparable management model. However, several factors support a lower multiple for
MTAL.

Most notably, the Queenstown business benefited from stronger contractual security, with management rights
extending through to December 2036, representing approximately 10 years of remaining tenure at the time of
sale. In contrast, while MTAL's Apartment Management Agreements contain stated terms of up to 30 years,
apartment owners retain the right to terminate on six months' notice without cause. As a result, the effective
contractual protection underpinning MTAL's earnings is materially weaker in substance than the Queenstown
comparable.

The Queenstown operation was also larger in scale, managing 19 apartments compared with MTAL's 14
apartments. In addition, two of MTAL's apartments currently operate without formal Management
Agreements, further reducing earnings security. Location is another differentiating factor. Queenstown
continues to attract a premium valuation position within the New Zealand tourism and accommodation sector,
reflecting its international profile, constrained supply dynamics, and strong investor demand. Commentary
provided in relation to the transaction noted that the 4.0x multiple was, in part, attributable to Queenstown's
location premium and may not have been achievable in other regional markets with a similar remaining
contract term.



                                                                                                                  27
Having regard to these differences, we consider it appropriate to apply a discount to the Queenstown
benchmark when assessing MTAL. This supports an indicative market-based multiple range of approximately
2.0x to 3.0x normalised EBITDA, with a midpoint of approximately 2.5x.



Management rights comparable transactions
As a further cross-check specific to MTAL’s own sector, we have considered recent management and letting
rights (MLR) transaction evidence from Australia and New Zealand. In the Australian market, which is the
deepest and most actively benchmarked MLR market, management rights businesses are valued by applying a
multiplier to verified net operating profit (NOP), with the multiplier typically ranging from 3.5 to 6.5 times
(MRM Finance; Ras360; Mike Phipps Finance; ResortBrokers Management Rights Report 2025). The multiplier
is, in substance, a years’-purchase figure (a multiplier of 5.0 equates to a 20% capitalisation rate) and is driven
principally by the length and security of the caretaking and letting agreements: agreements with 20-plus years
of secure tenure attract the upper end of the range, while shorter or less secure agreements are discounted.

New Zealand transaction evidence is thinner but directionally consistent. A 66-key Auckland CBD
management rights business currently marketed by LINK Business Brokers is priced at $1,050,000 against
maintainable profit of $300,000 — an implied multiple of approximately 3.5x — underpinned by a
management agreement secured to December 2044 (approximately 18 years of remaining tenure). The
Queenstown comparable already discussed above (19 apartments, $1.95m, approximately 4.0x, management
rights to December 2036) sits slightly higher, consistent with its longer secure tenure and Queenstown’s
location premium.

The relevant observation for MTAL is that these comparable multiples — a sector floor of around 3.5x rising to
6.5x — attach to businesses with long, secure contractual tenure (18–25 years in the cited comparables).
MTAL’s position is materially weaker: notwithstanding the 30-year stated term of the Apartment Management
Agreements, each owner may terminate on six months’ notice without cause under clause 7.6, giving an
effective tenure closer to six months per apartment; two of the fourteen apartments operate without any
formal agreement; and the business carries single-location and top-three revenue concentration
(approximately $0.610m of FY2026 letting revenue). These are precisely the security factors the sector
evidence identifies as the primary determinants of multiplier compression. Accordingly, MTAL is appropriately
assessed below the sector floor, supporting the 2.0x–3.0x normalised EBITDA range adopted in this cross-
check (mid 2.5x) ; on the post-tax FCFF basis, with the equity return calibrated as described, the DCF
reconciles with the lower end of this market range.

We note one definitional caveat: the Australian and New Zealand MLR multipliers above are struck on verified
NOP / sellers’ discretionary earnings, which are generally stated before deduction of a full arm’s-length
manager’s salary, whereas MTAL’s normalised EBITDA of $0.401m is already net of a $0.170m market-rate
general manager replacement cost. On a like-for-like (pre-manager) basis MTAL’s implied multiple would be
higher, meaning the 2.0x–3.0x range applied here is, if anything, conservative.

Appendix
In preparing this valuation, we have reviewed publicly observed transaction multiples and benchmark datasets
for hospitality, lodging and accommodation-services transactions. While none of these are directly
comparable to an owner-operated accommodation manager of MTAL’s scale and operating profile, they
provide a useful orientation of the multiples range typically associated with this sector. Comparable-
transactions analysis has not been relied upon as a primary valuation input — it is shown here as a cross-check
only — for the following reasons:

    -   Scale mismatch — publicly observed hospitality and accommodation services transactions are
        materially larger than MTAL (typically >A$50m EV) and are influenced by strategic, platform and
        synergy premia that do not apply to an owner-operated micro-SME;
    -   Owner-operator vs corporate/platform profile — MTAL is an owner-operated business, whereas the
        publicly reported hospitality transactions (e.g. SkyCity, Event Hospitality) are corporate and platform
        deals with professional management, diversified product mixes and capital-markets access;
    -   Single-location concentration — MTAL operates from a single Wanaka complex (14 apartments across
        seven buildings of Marina Terrace). No observed hospitality comparable reflects an equivalent single-
        location operating profile;
    -   Data opacity for small NZ hospitality deals — privately negotiated sales of small owner-operated
        accommodation managers are rarely disclosed. As a result, comparable multiples have been drawn
        from publicly available aggregate datasets (BizBuySell hospitality benchmarks) and larger listed
        transactions rather than from direct private-company comps.

For these reasons, the valuation of MTAL has been derived using a Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) approach.
The DCF method better captures the intrinsic value of the going-concern accommodation-management
business, its contractual coverage under the Body Corporate Property Management Agreement and the 12
formal and 2 informal Apartment Management Agreements, and its single-location operating profile.
