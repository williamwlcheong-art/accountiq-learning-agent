## Design Specificity Verdict

### LLM assessment

The first `/valuation` viewport feels authored for this product: its large valuation-specific headline, restrained navy palette, report-structure motif, human-review message and limitation language fit an indicative business-valuation offer. The journey becomes category-interchangeable after that. The preview is schematic rather than proof, later sections rely on familiar SaaS cards and checklists, and the transition to the generic auth card and legacy product shell feels like a marketing site attached to an internal tool.

### Deterministic scan

The detector found two warnings in `web/app/globals.css`: generic Arial typography at line 24 and a top accent border combined with rounded corners on the report preview around line 856. The typography warning reinforces the lack of visual distinctiveness after the hero. The border warning appears to be a false positive: in the live page the accent reads as an intentional report-cover motif.

### Visual overlays

No reliable user-visible overlay was available because the Browser surface exposed read-only evaluation and did not support DOM/script injection. The fallback evidence was responsive DOM measurement, screenshots, console logs and source corroboration. The valuation page had no horizontal overflow at 320px or 390px; the login/auth card overflowed by roughly 4px at 390px.

## Overall Impression

AccountIQ has a calm, credible offer page and unusually disciplined commercial-boundary copy, but the end-to-end product does not yet carry that confidence through registration, sensitive-file upload, the analyst-heavy intake, payment return, human review and repeat use. The single biggest opportunity is to make one valuation journey continuous, resumable and trustworthy from the first CTA through delivered report.

## What's Working

- “Indicative only,” “not financial advice,” human review and non-certified boundaries are repeated clearly without making the offer sound evasive.
- The confirmation and clarification states explain fee, sources, assumptions, reviewer step and when no payment was taken.
- Generation, retry, payment failure/refund and ready states use human language, and the landing page has good semantic structure, focus treatment and mobile layout.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Stripe success and cancellation can orphan the buyer

**Why it matters:** Immediately after the highest-anxiety, highest-value action, a paid buyer can see the initial upload screen and believe their payment and work were lost. A repeat start can create a second order.

**Fix:** Persist the report/order before redirecting; bind it to Stripe return URLs; interpret success/cancel query parameters; show explicit acknowledgement; restore the correct status; add complete-payment and view-status actions in the customer home.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] The product requests financial statements before earning enough trust

**Why it matters:** P&L and balance-sheet files are commercially sensitive, but the customer sees no approved price/GST, reviewer identity, turnaround, security/data-use, retention/deletion, refund or support explanation before upload.

**Fix:** Put the approved purchase and data-handling facts before upload. Add a named reviewer, redacted sample report, company identity, policies and a human support path.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The intake is an analyst worksheet presented as one giant form

**Why it matters:** One screen asks an SME owner to manage narrative risk, adjustments, forecast assumptions, investment decisions and 40 risk-rating choices. This creates fatigue, competence anxiety, inaccurate answers and abandonment.

**Fix:** Split the intake into short saved steps, show total progress and time, translate jargon inline, prefill recommendations, hide optional advanced inputs until needed, and provide save-and-finish-later.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] The acquisition CTA loses intent at the account gate

**Why it matters:** “Get a valuation” opens “Sign in to AccountIQ.” First-time visitors have to discover a secondary registration control and the confident marketing presentation disappears.

**Fix:** Use `/signup?intent=business-valuation`, preserve the offer and next step on the auth surface, and route registration directly into the valuation flow.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Returning users receive a purchase table, not a customer home

**Why it matters:** The product behaves like a one-off download. There is no active-order timeline, draft resume, payment resume, company reuse, support, invoice, security, data-control or repeat-workflow surface.

**Fix:** Build a lightweight order/report dashboard led by the active valuation. Do not turn this into the out-of-scope financial-ratio dashboard.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** “Get a valuation” opens a sign-in form. After registration, Jordan sees five products although four do not work, then encounters CAGR, terminal growth, normalisations and operating working capital without contextual help. There is no step count or time estimate.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** At 390px the auth card extends slightly beyond the viewport. The risk ratings create a long mobile scroll, and an interruption before checkout loses the unsaved intake.

**Riley (Stress Tester):** Refreshing during intake loses the form. Cancelling or completing Stripe can return to a clean upload screen. Status-poll errors other than authentication are swallowed, leaving stale state without an explanation.

**Morgan (Privacy-Conscious Owner):** The upload request arrives before any privacy, retention, deletion, AI-processing, reviewer-access or support details. There is no public policy or named accountable reviewer to verify.

## Minor Observations

- The auth card overflows by about 4px at 390px because its viewport-relative max width does not account for container padding.
- Header sign-in and CTA controls are below the recommended 44px mobile touch target.
- The visible report preview is `aria-hidden`, although much of its content is repeated later.
- Business name should be marked required in the rendered control.
- Form errors should focus and identify the affected field instead of appearing only in a top alert.
- Registration lacks password recovery and terms/privacy acknowledgement.
- The footer lacks legal, privacy, support, contact and company identity.
- The four disabled future products make the valuation-only MVP feel unfinished.
- “Financial intelligence” in the authenticated header is less specific than the public business-valuation promise.

## Questions to Consider

- If the fee is fixed, what trust or conversion objective is served by hiding it until after financial statements are uploaded?
- Is AccountIQ selling one excellent valuation product or signalling five unfinished products?
- What must an owner believe before handing AccountIQ their P&L and balance sheet, and where is each belief proven?
- Should the owner choose valuation methodology inputs, or should AccountIQ translate plain business answers into recommended assumptions for confirmation?
- Is the desired end state a downloaded PDF or a lightweight ongoing valuation workspace?
