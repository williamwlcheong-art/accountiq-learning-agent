## Why this exists

Most business owners cannot easily find out what their business is worth.

A proper valuation from an advisory firm runs into thousands of dollars and
takes weeks, which makes sense when a deal is on the table and is hard to
justify when you are only trying to work out whether one is worth pursuing. The
free calculators online are the other extreme. They ask for a revenue figure and
an industry, multiply the two, and give you a number with no working behind it.

Owners get stuck between those two, usually at the moment a decision is
approaching: a buyer has made contact, a shareholder wants out, a bank has asked
a question.

AccountIQ sits in the middle. It reads your actual financial statements rather
than a single number you type in, asks about the things that change a valuation,
and produces a report that shows its assumptions so you can argue with them.

## How the reports are made

Software prepares the first draft and a person reviews it before release. That
order matters. The software is faster and more consistent at pulling figures out
of statements and applying a method to them. A person is better at noticing when
the result does not make sense.

No report reaches a customer without that review.

## What AccountIQ will not claim

The reports are indicative. They are a starting point for a decision or a
conversation, not financial advice, and not a certified or court-standard
valuation. Every report says so on its face.

Being straight about that is deliberate. An owner who acts on an overstated
number is worse off than one who never asked the question.
