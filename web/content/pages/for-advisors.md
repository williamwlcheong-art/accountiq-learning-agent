Clients ask what their business is worth more often than most firms have time to
answer properly. The work is not hard so much as slow: pulling the accounts
apart, agreeing the normalisations, building a model, writing it up.

AccountIQ does the first pass of that work and hands you a draft with its
assumptions on the page.

## How firms use it

Some order a report themselves, using the client's statements, and use it as the
starting point for their own advice. Others point the client at it directly and
pick up the conversation once the report exists.

Either way the fee is fixed and known before anything is prepared, so you can
tell a client what it costs without a scoping exercise.

## The assumptions are visible

Every figure that was calculated rather than supplied is shown as such, with the
period it came from. Where a client overrode a calculated figure, the report
carries their reason.

That matters for your own file. You are not handed a number with no working,
and you can disagree with a specific assumption rather than the whole report.

## A person reviews every report

No report reaches a client straight off the software. A reviewer checks the
draft against the statements before release. The report still carries the
limits it should: indicative, not advice, and not a certified or court-standard
valuation.
