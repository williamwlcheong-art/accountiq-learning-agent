## What to have ready

Your last two to three years of annual financial statements, as PDF or Excel
files. Exports from Xero, MYOB or your accountant's software all work. Older
accounts are fine to include, and more history usually gives a better result.

It also helps to have thought about two things before you start. First, any
one-off costs or income in those years that a buyer would not expect to see
again: an owner's salary above or below market rate, a one-off legal bill, a
personal vehicle. Second, what you expect revenue to do over the next few years
and why.

## Create an account and upload

Registration takes an email address and a password. Once you are in, you upload
the statements and give the business a name.

AccountIQ reads the statements and pulls out the figures it needs: revenue,
earnings, depreciation, working capital and the balance sheet. It then shows you
what it found, so you can see the numbers it is working from before you go any
further.

## Answer the questions

The questions cover the business itself and the assumptions behind the
valuation. Some are about what you do and who you sell to. Others ask you to
confirm or correct figures that were worked out from your statements, such as
depreciation as a share of revenue.

Where a figure was calculated for you, you can accept it or replace it with your
own and say why. Those reasons are recorded and appear in the report, so the
assumptions are visible rather than buried.

## See the fee and pay

The fee is fixed and shown in full before you pay anything. Payment goes through
Stripe. AccountIQ never sees your card details.

## The report is prepared and reviewed

The first draft is prepared from your figures and your answers. It is not sent
to you at that point.

A reviewer reads the draft, checks the assumptions against the statements you
uploaded, and looks at whether the valuation range holds up. If something does
not, it goes back before release rather than out to you.

## You get the report

When the report is released you get an email, and the report appears in your
account. You can read it online or download it as a PDF. It stays in your
account, so you can come back to it later.

## What the report is not

It is an indicative valuation, meant to inform a decision or a conversation. It
is not financial advice, and it is not a certified, official or court-standard
valuation. If you need one of those, you need a separate engagement with a
registered valuer, and the report says so.
