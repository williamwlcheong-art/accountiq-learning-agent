# SEO and content plan

Date: 2026-09-16
Revised: 2026-09-16, after four independent reviews and a competitor check.

Search volume and keyword difficulty come from DataForSEO (Google Ads volume
and live SERP), pulled on 2026-09-16 for New Zealand. Competitor prices were
read off the competitors' own published pages on the same day and are quoted
below with the date, because they will change.

## Read this part first

The first version of this plan rested on one claim: that nobody in New Zealand
publishes a price for a business valuation, so publishing ours would be the
wedge. That claim is false, and it was checkable from the list of page-one
results in the plan's own research.

Three competitors on that list publish prices.

| Who | Price (ex GST) | Turnaround | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bizstats | $60 per report, $45 at volume | Instant | Comparable sales data from real NZ broker transactions. Does not read your accounts. |
| Bizval | $2,250 under $2m turnover, $3,400 to $10m, $4,500 above, $5,200 court standard | 7 to 21 working days | A report compiled by a chartered accountant. Fast track costs $850 to $1,400 more. |
| Fealty | $4,900 shareholder or pre-sale, $9,900 dispute | 3 to 8 weeks | Certified Valuation Analyst report, court and IRD grade. |
| Business brokers | Free | Days | An appraisal given away to win the listing. Conflicted by design. |

Fealty's home page title is "Business Valuation NZ | Fixed-Price Independent
Valuer". Bizval's home page says every report is compiled by a qualified
chartered accountant at a fixed-rate fee. Both of AccountIQ's headline claims
are already made by a competitor that ranks for the target term.

So the plan below is rewritten around what is actually open.

That table sums to 780, not the 1,000 claimed in the first version. Inside the

people counted twice, and the three calculator rows are largely one query.
Unique demand is closer to 450 to 550 searches a month.

### Difficulty zero does not mean the door is open

Keyword difficulty scores the links pointing at the pages currently ranking. It
says nothing about the authority of the sites behind them. A score of zero
sitting next to Grant Thornton, ANZ and Xero means those firms rank with thin
pages that nobody links to, which is useful, and it does not mean a new domain
can walk past them.

A business valuation is a money question, where Google weights site history
heavily. Page one on the head New Zealand terms is a six to nine month outcome,
not a three month one.

### What the research missed

City terms were never pulled. New Zealand service searches usually carry a city,
those results show map packs, and a Google Business Profile costs nothing. Pull
volumes for Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch and Tauranga before the next
round of pages.

## What has to be true before any of this is published

`.planning/commercial/LAUNCH-GATES.md` says all eight launch gates are open, and
that AccountIQ must not accept public users or public Stripe payments while any
gate is open. Three of them block parts of this plan directly.

- **Gate 2** has not decided what the review claim may say, or whether CA ANZ
  marks can be used. That blocks every page that mentions the review, which is
  most of them.
- **Gate 3** has not decided whether uploaded statements go to an offshore AI
  provider. That blocks the page explaining where statements go, which is the
  page that earns the upload.
- **Gate 5** has no GST treatment and no refund path in the database. That
  blocks publishing a fee and blocks the Offer schema.

As written today, this plan would build traffic toward a checkout that is not
allowed to open. Closing those three gates comes before publishing, not after.

## The strategic read

**The gap is the middle of the market, and speed.** Between a $60 data pull that
never reads your accounts and a $2,250 report that takes seven working days,
there is nothing. AccountIQ reads the actual statements and can return a report
in days. That is the position: cheaper than the accountant's report, and it
reads more than the data report does.

**The fee has to be between $950 and $1,500.** Two constraints bound it. Above
roughly $1,500 an owner stops deciding alone and rings their accountant, and
accounting firms win conversations. Below roughly $500 nobody believes a
chartered accountant opened the accounts and signed off, which destroys the one
claim worth having. Published at $1,200 against Bizval's $2,250 and Fealty's
$4,900, the number reads as a discount off a real service. Gate 5 has to settle
GST before the number can be stated as including it.

**Search is the credibility layer, not the channel.** A valuation is bought at a
trigger event, and at that moment an owner rings their accountant rather than
opening Google. The people who do search are early and not buying, or have no
accountant worth ringing, or have been quoted five figures and are price
shopping. Only the third group is a good customer. William is a chartered
accountant with a professional network, and ten phone calls to accountants and
brokers will answer whether this product has distribution faster than six months
of publishing will.

## The pricing page

This is the most valuable page on the site and it is one page, not a page plus a
blog post competing with it.

It answers the cost question as a comparison, naming the real alternatives and
their real prices: a free broker appraisal and why it is conflicted, a $60
Bizstats data report and what it cannot tell you, a $2,250 Bizval report and how
long it takes, a $4,900 Fealty certified valuation and when you actually need
one. Then AccountIQ's number, what is included, the turnaround in days, and who
it is not for.

The People Also Ask questions become the headings, worded exactly as people
search them. FAQPage schema goes on this page and nowhere else.

## The calculator: not this year

The first version proposed building a free calculator. Do not build it.

Free New Zealand calculators already exist at `calculator.fealty.co.nz` and on
Bizstats, and Bizstats' estimator is backed by real transaction data that
AccountIQ does not have. Building a seventh revenue-multiple calculator buys a
ranking for a string, not an advantage.

The deeper problem is that AccountIQ's own draft about page argues against
exactly this tool, saying free calculators take a revenue figure and an industry,
multiply them, and give a number with no working behind it. The proposed
calculator did that.

If a free tool is ever built, the only version worth building takes an uploaded
set of statements rather than four typed numbers, because that is the actual
product and no competitor can copy it. Revisit after the fee is set and the
gates close.

Two posts from the first version are gone. The cost post is now the body of the

outranks the page with the buy button on it. The EBITDA explainer is cut: 1,600

## Trust, which the first version left out entirely

A stranger is being asked to upload their annual financial statements and pay for
a document they have never seen, from a brand they have never heard of. That is
the conversion barrier. The keyword is not.

Four things, all of which outperform any blog post:

- **William named.** Photo, CA ANZ membership, where he trained and practised, on
  the home page and next to every mention of money. "A reviewer checks the
  report" reads like a factory QA step. An anonymous reviewer reads as an
  offshore contractor and is worse than saying nothing. Gate 2 has to settle what
  the claim may say first.
- **A full sample report**, every page of it, for a made-up New Zealand company,
  on its own URL, downloadable with no email wall. Bizstats already offers one.
- **Three named customers** saying one sentence each about whether it was worth
  the money.
- **A plain page on data handling**: where statements are stored, who can open
  them, which provider processes them, when they are deleted, and whether there
  is professional indemnity cover. Gate 3 has to settle this first.

## Two things the shipped site contradicts

The valuation page currently says the fixed fee is shown before payment, after
you create an account and complete the valuation information. To an owner, a
price shown after a signup and a form is a quote, and a quote is what they came
to Google to avoid. Those sentences have to go, and the number goes in the
heading instead.

The same page says the product is built for New Zealand and Australian SMEs. The
whole advantage here is being conspicuously local. Say New Zealand.

## Where the multiples come from

This was a tick box in the first version. It is a gate.

Bizstats has more than 12,500 real New Zealand transactions behind its numbers.
Bizval publishes its own indicator and explains how it is built. If AccountIQ
publishes a multiple with no stated source, the first accountant who reads it in
a client's report discounts the product, and accountants are the second audience
this plan names.

Write one sentence saying where the ranges come from, good enough to sit in the
report and on the site. Publish no multiple until that sentence exists.

## Answer engines

Both target SERPs carry an AI Overview, so there is a trade-off the first version
ignored: being quoted and being clicked are not the same win.

"How much does a business valuation cost in NZ" is a question an AI Overview
answers completely in one line, and AccountIQ would be supplying the only
published number on the page. So aim the quotable passages at the questions where
being cited helps, and make the price answer incomplete without the visit by
publishing the fee alongside the turnaround, what is included and who it is not
for.

Connect Bing Webmaster Tools as well as Search Console, because ChatGPT search
leans on Bing's index.

## Measurement

Two lines at the top of the scoreboard, because a plan that ranks first and sells
nothing should not read as a success:

- Reports sold where the first touch was organic
- Hours spent per report sold

Then rankings as a leading indicator, on a six month horizon rather than three.

**Kill condition, written down now.** If organic has produced fewer than three
paid reports by month six, the channel is answered and the effort moves to
referral.

Check Fealty's and Bizval's published prices monthly. In a market where the
position is price, a competitor changing their number changes the pricing page
overnight.

## Order of work

1. Set the fee between $950 and $1,500, and settle its GST treatment.
2. Close Gates 2, 3 and 5.
3. Write the pricing page as the comparison above.
4. Publish the sample report, and put William's name, credentials and photo next

Roughly three weeks of work, for one to four reports a month by month six.
