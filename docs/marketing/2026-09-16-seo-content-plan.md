### Who currently ranks

For "business valuation nz": Grant Thornton, CA ANZ, ANZ, Baker Tilly, Xero,
Proficio, Bizval, RSM, Bizstats.

For "how to value a business": BNZ, ANZ, Xero, LegalVision, Investopedia.

Two things follow. The accounting firms rank on brand and never publish a price.
The banks and Xero rank with general explainer articles written for a global or
trans-Tasman audience. Neither group is defending the specific New Zealand
valuation terms with pages built for them.

### The questions Google is already surfacing

Both money terms show an AI Overview, and the People Also Ask boxes contain:

- How do I calculate the valuation of my business?
- **How much does a valuation cost in NZ?**
- **How much should a business valuation cost?**
- How much is a business worth with $1 million in sales?
- How many times earnings is a business worth?
- How much is my business worth in NZ?
- How much is a business worth based on turnover?

Two of the seven are about cost. No one on page one answers them, because
advisory firms quote privately.

## The strategic read

**The wedge is the published price.** AccountIQ's differentiator and the
single most asked unanswered question on this SERP are the same thing. A
pricing page that states a fixed fee in plain numbers can own a question that
Grant Thornton and Baker Tilly will never answer. This moves the fee from a
nice-to-have to the highest-value missing input we have.

**The biggest single sub-cluster is a tool, not an article.** The calculator
variants add up to around 250 searches a month with difficulty between 0 and 14.
People searching for a calculator want to type numbers in and see a figure, and
they will not be satisfied by a page explaining that valuation is complicated. A
free calculator is the right answer to that intent, and it is also the natural
top of our funnel.

**Organic search alone will not fill a pipeline.** Roughly 1,000 direct searches
a month, at a realistic 20 to 30 percent share of clicks once we rank, is 200 to
300 visitors a month. This plan should be judged as a way to capture high intent
demand cheaply and to build credibility, not as a growth engine on its own.
Whoever is doing business development should hear that plainly.

## The calculator

Build `/business-valuation-calculator` as a real, free, no-signup tool.

The user enters revenue, an earnings figure, an owner's salary adjustment and an
industry. The tool returns a range, not a single number, and shows the multiple
it used and why. It then says plainly what it cannot know: customer
concentration, owner dependence, the quality of the records. The call to action
is the paid report, positioned as the version that reads the actual statements.

This is worth building for three reasons. It answers the highest-volume
sub-cluster at near-zero difficulty. It gives the site something linkable, which
is the only realistic way to earn links in this niche. And a visitor who has
typed their own numbers in is a far better lead than one who read an article.

The risk is that a free tool cannibalises the paid report. It does not, if the
tool is honest about its own limits: a revenue multiple with no normalisation is
a starting point, and saying so is both true and the strongest argument for
paying.

## Blog plan

Five posts, each aimed at a question the data shows people ask. Every post links
to the valuation page and to the calculator once it exists.

| # | Working title | Target | Monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is my business actually worth? | how much is my business worth / business valuation nz | 120 |
| 2 | What does a business valuation cost in New Zealand? | valuation cost questions | PAA |
| 3 | What multiple does a business sell for? | how many times earnings is a business worth | PAA |
| 4 | EBITDA, explained for business owners | ebitda | 1,600 |
| 5 | What buyers look at before they look at your profit | buying a business nz | 480 |

Post 1 is drafted. Post 2 matters more than its volume suggests, because it
answers the unanswered SERP question and sends people to the pricing page. Post
4 is the volume play: "ebitda" is 1,600 searches a month at low competition, and
most results are American. A New Zealand explainer that uses a New Zealand
example is a fair fight.

Two topics from the earlier list are dropped. "Why profit and value differ" is
covered inside posts 1 and 4. "How to prepare accounts before a sale" shows only
10 searches a month, so it becomes a section of post 5 rather than its own page.

## Answer engines

Both target SERPs already show an AI Overview, so the pages need to be quotable
by a model, not only readable by a person.

- Answer the question in the first two sentences under each heading, then
  explain. Models lift short, self-contained passages.
- Use the question as the heading, worded the way people search it.
- Put the fee, when we have it, in plain text near a heading that contains the
  word cost. Do not put it only in an image or a table cell.
- Keep the disclaimer language nearby, so a model quoting the price also picks
  up that the report is indicative.

## Schema

Already planned in the site spec, with two additions from this research:

- `FAQPage` on the pricing page using the exact People Also Ask wording.
- `HowTo` on the how it works page.
- `SoftwareApplication` plus `Offer` on the calculator page, carrying the fee.

## Measurement

Search Console is not connected. That is the first thing to fix, because none of
this is verifiable without it. Then track, monthly:

- Impressions and clicks for the nine terms in the first table
- Position for "business valuation nz" and "business valuation calculator nz"
- Calculator completions, and what share of them register

A fair first target is ranking on page one for the four zero-difficulty New
Zealand terms within three months of publishing, since nothing currently defends
them.

## What this plan cannot do

It cannot manufacture demand that is not there. About 1,000 people a month in
New Zealand search for a business valuation. Winning all of them is a good
outcome and a small number. If the business needs more than a few hundred
visitors a month, the answer is accountant referral partnerships, the advisor
channel, or paid search against "business broker nz" and "business for sale nz",
and none of those are SEO.
